PCB Design Engineer

🧑‍🎨 What Do They Do?

  • Have you ever seen a green board inside a mobile phone, remote control, or any electronic devices?
  • This board helps all the electronic parts work together.
  • This green board is called a Printed Circuit Board (PCB).
  • The person who designs these boards is called a PCB Design Engineer.
  • 👉 In simple words: PCB Design Engineers design the circuit boards used inside electronic devices.

🎓 How to Become a PCB Design Engineer

You have two options:

  • Start early with a Diploma
    After Class 10, you can choose a relevant diploma course. Complete the diploma and join the second year of an engineering course by applying through lateral entry.
  • Finish Class 12
    Complete Class 11 and 12, then you can do a B.Tech./B.E. in the relevant field.

Where do PCB Design Engineers work? (Job Roles + Growth)

PCB Design Engineers work in : Electronics companies, Semiconductor companies, Consumer electronics companies, Automotive companies, Aerospace companies, Medical device companies, Telecommunications companies, and Industrial automation companies.

🟢 Beginner Roles

Junior PCB Layout Engineer

(0–2 Years Experience)

Designs basic PCB layouts using CAD software.

Hardware Test Engineer

(0–2 Years Experience)

Tests circuit boards and finds hardware issues.

🔵 Mid-Level Roles

PCB Design Engineer

(2–5 Years Experience)

Designs complex multi-layer circuit boards.

Hardware Design Engineer

(3–5 Years Experience)

Creates circuit designs for electronic products.

🟣 Advanced Roles

Senior Hardware Architect

(5+ Years Experience)

Designs advanced electronic systems and boards.

VP of Hardware Systems

(8+ Years Experience)

Leads teams developing electronic products.

Consumer Electronics Companies

Design PCBs for smartphones, laptops, and smart devices.

Automotive Technology Companies

Create PCBs used in modern vehicles and EVs.

Industrial Automation Companies

Design circuit boards for machines and factories.

Medical Equipment Companies

Develop PCBs for medical devices and equipment.

Aerospace & Defense Organizations

Build PCBs for aircraft, satellites, and defense systems.

Electronics Research Organizations

Create advanced PCB technologies for future products.
