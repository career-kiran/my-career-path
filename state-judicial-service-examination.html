State Judicial Service Examination

State Judicial Service Examination is a competitive examination conducted to select candidates for entry-level judicial posts such as Civil Judge / Judicial Magistrate, according to the rules of the respective state.

This examination is mainly for law graduates who want to build a career in the judiciary and work as a judge in the courts of their state.

Conducted by: Each state has its own Judicial Service Examination, conducted by the state's designated recruiting authority.

📌 Quick Facts

Eligibility: Usually requires an LL.B. degree. Other requirements, such as age, practice experience, and language requirements, may vary by state.

Exam Pattern: Varies by state. It may include Preliminary Exam, Main Exam, and Interview.

Mode: Online (Computer-Based Test) or Offline (Pen-and-Paper) (varies by state).

Frequency: Depends on the vacancies and recruitment schedule of each state.

Important: Each state has its own Judicial Service Examination and recruitment rules. Always check the latest notification of the concerned state before applying.

Qualification:

  • Must hold an LL.B. degree from a recognized university.
  • Other academic requirements may vary depending on the state.

Practice Experience:

  • For recruitment notifications issued up to March 31, 2027, law graduates can apply without prior legal practice under the transitional arrangement.
  • For recruitment notifications issued from April 1, 2027 onwards, candidates will need at least 1 year of actual legal practice.
  • From April 1, 2027 onwards, candidates have at least 1 year of experience as a practicing advocate. They must provide proof of this practice when applying.

Age Limit:

  • The age limit varies by state.
  • Age relaxations may be available according to the rules of the respective state.

Other Requirements:

  • Some states may have additional requirements, such as knowledge of the local language.
  • Always check the latest notification issued by the concerned state before applying.

Stages of Examination:

  • Preliminary Examination: Objective-type examination, usually consisting of Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs).
  • Main Examination: Written/descriptive examination covering various areas of law and other subjects prescribed by the state.
  • Interview/Viva-Voce: Candidates who qualify the written examinations may be called for an interview or viva-voce.

Mode of Examination:

  • Online (Computer-Based Test)
  • Offline (Pen-and-Paper)
  • (Varies by state)

Question Type:

  • Objective-type questions
  • Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs)
  • Descriptive/written questions in the Main Examination
  • (Varies by state)

Subjects:

  • Constitutional Law
  • Civil Law
  • Criminal Law
  • Other subjects prescribed by the respective state
  • (Varies by state)

Duration: Varies by state and examination stage.

Total Questions: Varies by state and examination stage.

Total Marks: Varies by state and examination stage.

Marking Scheme:

  • Marks for correct answers vary by state.
  • Negative marking may apply in some states.
  • (Varies by state)

Important:

  • The examination pattern, syllabus, marking scheme, and duration are decided by the respective state examination authority or High Court. Candidates should always check the latest official notification of their state.
